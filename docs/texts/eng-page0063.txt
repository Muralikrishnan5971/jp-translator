Chapter 25: Baby's Bedding and Pillow (赤ちゃんの寝具と枕)
1. Does a baby need a special bed?
- The book explains that in some homes, there may not be enough space for a separate baby bed.
- Even in such homes, it is possible to prepare a safe sleeping place for the baby.
- A special baby bed is not always necessary.
- The important thing is to make sure the baby sleeps in a safe environment.
2. Traditional Japanese baby bedding
- In Japan, there is a traditional custom of preparing special bedding for newborn babies.
- The book mentions a traditional type of baby futon called Shōni-yō futon (小児用布団).
- Parents and grandparents often prepare this bedding before the baby is born.
- The author explains that some traditional practices are not necessarily good for babies.
- Parents should consider the baby's health and comfort rather than simply following customs.
3. Avoid making the baby's bedding too soft
- Parents and grandparents naturally want babies to sleep comfortably.
- Because of this, they sometimes prepare very soft and thick bedding.
- However, the author warns that excessively soft bedding can be dangerous.
- A baby's body may sink into very soft bedding.
- The book recommends avoiding bedding that is too soft.
4. The problem with cotton bedding
- Traditional Japanese baby futons were often made using cotton.
- Cotton bedding can absorb moisture.
- Over time, cotton may become compressed and lose its original shape.
- The author discusses how cotton bedding can become uneven after repeated use.
- Parents should pay attention to the condition of the bedding.
5. Choosing a suitable mattress
- The book recommends choosing bedding that supports the baby's body properly.
- A mattress should not be excessively soft.
- It should remain reasonably flat when the baby lies on it.
- The author discusses using materials that maintain their shape.
- The book mentions bedding made from synthetic materials, including quilted or padded materials.
6. The importance of cleanliness
- The baby's bedding should be kept clean.
- New bedding should be checked before use.
- The author warns against bedding that sheds loose fibres.
- Loose fibres and dust can make the sleeping environment uncomfortable.
- Bedding should be maintained so that it remains hygienic.
7. Does a newborn need a pillow?
- The author explains that a newborn baby does not need a pillow.
- A baby's head and body are shaped differently from those of an adult.
- A thick pillow is unnecessary.
- The author recommends avoiding high pillows for newborn babies.
8. Using a folded towel instead of a pillow
- The book suggests that a folded towel can be used in place of a pillow.
- A towel can be folded into a low, flat support.
- It is easy to wash and replace.
- The author considers this more practical than purchasing an expensive baby pillow.
9. Why low pillows are preferred
- Babies have relatively large heads compared with their bodies.
- A high pillow may place the baby's neck in an uncomfortable position.
- A low pillow or no pillow is considered preferable.
- The author emphasizes that expensive bedding is not necessarily better.
10. Preparing bedding before the baby arrives
- Parents often receive bedding as gifts when a baby is born.
- The book suggests examining these gifts before using them.
- Parents should choose bedding based on usefulness and safety.
- Decorative or expensive items are not always necessary.
- Simple, practical bedding is often sufficient.

The author emphasizes that babies do not need expensive or elaborate bedding. Simple, clean, firm bedding is more important.

The main points are:
1. A separate baby bed may not always be necessary, but the sleeping place must be safe.
2. Avoid excessively soft mattresses.
3. Choose bedding that provides proper support.
4. Keep the baby's bedding clean.
5. Newborn babies do not need thick pillows.
6. The book suggests using a folded towel instead of an expensive pillow.
7. Choose practical bedding rather than decorative bedding.
Modern safety note: Although the book suggests a folded towel as a pillow, current infant safe-sleep recommendations advise no pillow or folded towel beneath a sleeping baby's head. Babies should sleep on their backs on a firm, flat, appropriately fitted mattress with only a fitted sheet, in their own safety-approved sleep space.
