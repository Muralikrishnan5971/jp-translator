Chapter 15: Ultrasound Examination (Echo)
Japanese title: 超音波による検査（エコー）
1. Checking blood flow in the placenta
- A Doppler ultrasound machine can examine blood flow in the placenta.
- This helps doctors understand how blood is circulating between the mother and the placenta.
- It can also help doctors identify certain pregnancy-related problems.
2. Detecting high blood pressure during pregnancy
- The author explains that, around the 20th week of pregnancy, doctors can use Doppler ultrasound to help predict pregnancy-related high blood pressure.
- Doctors may use this information to identify women who need closer monitoring.
- The book describes this as an important development in prenatal care.
3. High blood pressure and childbirth
- Pregnancy-related high blood pressure can be dangerous for the mother and baby.
- According to the book, delivering the baby and placenta is an important part of treating the condition.
- The author states that blood pressure usually improves within approximately 10 days after delivery.
- However, the time needed for recovery can vary.
4. When can childbirth be safely performed?
- The author says that mild high blood pressure, around 160/110 mmHg, may sometimes be managed until delivery.
- However, some doctors recommend delivering the baby sooner.
- The author describes a doctor who recommends early delivery rather than waiting for the pregnancy to continue.
- This doctor discusses the timing of childbirth with both the mother and father.
- The book emphasizes cooperation between the doctor and the parents when planning delivery.
5. How ultrasound helps doctors
- Ultrasound allows doctors to observe the baby inside the uterus.
- It helps doctors monitor the baby's growth and development.
- The author explains that ultrasound has greatly improved prenatal examinations.
- Doctors can now observe details that were previously difficult to examine.
6. Examining the baby's growth
- Earlier, doctors could mainly judge fetal growth by looking at the baby's head.
- Ultrasound makes it possible to measure different parts of the baby's body.
- Doctors can measure the baby's head and abdominal size.
- They can also measure the length of the thigh bone (femur).
- These measurements help doctors estimate the baby's growth.
7. Detecting abnormalities in the baby
- Ultrasound can help identify certain abnormalities before birth.
- For example, doctors can examine the baby's head and abdomen.
- An unusually large head or abdomen may indicate a medical problem.
- The book mentions hydrocephalus, a condition involving excess fluid in the brain.
- Ultrasound can also help doctors detect certain problems with the baby's digestive system.
8. Checking the baby's internal organs
- Doctors can examine the baby's organs using ultrasound.
- The author mentions that ultrasound can help identify abnormalities that may need treatment after birth.
- Some problems can be recognized before the baby is delivered.
- This allows doctors to prepare appropriate medical care.
9. Measuring amniotic fluid
- Ultrasound can measure the amount of amniotic fluid surrounding the baby.
- Amniotic fluid helps protect the developing baby.
- Doctors can identify whether there is too much or too little amniotic fluid.
- The amount of fluid can provide important information about the pregnancy.
10. Checking the placenta
- Ultrasound helps doctors determine the position of the placenta.
- Doctors can examine its size and maturity.
- This information helps them understand whether the pregnancy is progressing normally.
- The author explains that these examinations have made childbirth safer.
11. Determining the baby's position
- Before ultrasound became common, doctors sometimes had difficulty identifying the baby's exact position.
- A baby may be positioned head-down or bottom-down.
- Ultrasound allows doctors to identify the baby's position more accurately.
- The book mentions that babies may naturally change position during pregnancy.
- If the baby is not positioned head-down, doctors can monitor the situation.
12. Improvements in maternity care
- Ultrasound has reduced uncertainty in pregnancy examinations.
- Doctors can observe fetal development rather than relying only on external examinations.
- It helps identify some complications before childbirth.
- The author explains that ultrasound has become an important tool in modern obstetrics.

Ultrasound is an important medical examination during pregnancy. It allows doctors to:
1. Check blood flow in the placenta.
2. Monitor the baby's growth.
3. Measure the baby's head, abdomen, and thigh bone.
4. Detect certain fetal abnormalities.
5. Measure amniotic fluid.
6. Check the placenta's position and development.
7. Identify the baby's position before delivery.
8. Plan medical care when complications are detected.
Medical note: This is a simplified translation of the book's content, not current clinical guidance. In particular, a blood pressure of 160/110 mmHg during pregnancy is considered severe and requires urgent medical assessment. Ultrasound and Doppler examinations can help assess pregnancy complications, but they cannot reliably predict or detect every condition.
