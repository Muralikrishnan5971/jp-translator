Chapter 16: Things to Avoid During Pregnancy (妊娠中してはいけないこと)
1. Medicines during pregnancy
- Medicines taken by the mother can sometimes affect the developing baby.
- The first three months of pregnancy are especially important because the baby's organs are forming.
- Some medicines can interfere with the baby's development during this period.
- The author gives examples of medicines that may affect the baby's nerves.
- Pregnant women should be careful about taking medicines without medical advice.
2. Medicines that affect the baby's nervous system
- Some medicines can act on the baby's nervous system.
- The author explains that medicines taken by the mother may affect the baby developing inside the uterus.
- The text gives the example of medicines that can interfere with nerve function.
- These medicines may be particularly concerning during early pregnancy.
- However, not every medicine causes harm to the baby.
3. Taking medicine for headaches
- Some pregnant women experience headaches.
- The author says that headaches may be caused by pregnancy-related changes.
- A headache does not always indicate a serious medical condition.
- However, pregnant women should not automatically take medicine without considering the cause.
- The author recommends consulting a doctor when necessary.
4. Medicines for shoulder stiffness and headaches
- Some women experience shoulder stiffness or headaches during pregnancy.
- The author mentions that doctors may prescribe medicines to relieve these symptoms.
- However, the safety of some medicines during pregnancy may not be fully established.
- The author advises caution when taking muscle relaxants or other medicines.
- Women should ask their doctors whether a medicine is suitable during pregnancy.
5. Understanding medicine risks
- The author explains that avoiding every medicine during pregnancy is not always necessary.
- Sometimes, an untreated illness may be more dangerous than the medicine used to treat it.
- Doctors must consider the benefits and risks of treatment.
- The author says that medical decisions should be based on the mother's condition.
- Pregnant women should not stop necessary treatment without medical advice.
6. Consulting doctors during pregnancy
- The author recommends seeking medical advice before taking unfamiliar medicines.
- Doctors should consider the possibility of pregnancy when prescribing treatment.
- Women should tell their doctors if they are pregnant or planning pregnancy.
- Medicines should be used only when their expected benefits justify the risks.
- The author emphasizes that proper medical care is important.

Main message of this page
The first part discusses medicine safety during pregnancy, particularly medicines that may affect the developing baby's nervous system. The author emphasizes the importance of weighing the benefits and risks of treatment.

The second part introduces the Japanese custom of Satogaeri Bunben, in which pregnant women return to their parents' home for childbirth. It discusses family support, planning the delivery location, and the husband's responsibilities.

Important medical context: Many medicines can be used safely during pregnancy when medically indicated. The first trimester is an important period for fetal development, but medication risks can also exist later in pregnancy. Pregnant women should consult their healthcare professional before starting or stopping prescribed medicines.
