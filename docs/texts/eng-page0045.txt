Chapter 11: Minor Problems During Pregnancy (Continued)
1. Constipation during pregnancy
- Constipation is a common problem during pregnancy.
- Some women may feel embarrassed to discuss it with their doctor.
- However, doctors, especially obstetricians, are familiar with this problem.
- The author explains that constipation may sometimes cause hemorrhoids (piles).
- Hemorrhoids can make childbirth and recovery more uncomfortable.
- Pregnant women should not ignore severe constipation.
2. Insomnia during pregnancy
- Some women experience difficulty sleeping during early pregnancy.
- This can be caused by changes in the body.
- Pregnant women may also feel anxious about their pregnancy.
- The author says that insomnia is usually not harmful to the baby.
- However, women should avoid taking sleeping pills without medical advice.
- The author also warns against trying new sleeping medicines during pregnancy.
3. Nosebleeds during pregnancy
- Some pregnant women experience nosebleeds.
- Pregnancy increases blood flow in different parts of the body.
- The blood vessels inside the nose may become more sensitive.
- This can make nosebleeds more likely.
- The author recommends gently pressing the nose to stop the bleeding.
- The text suggests that occasional nosebleeds are usually not serious.
4. Dental problems during pregnancy
- Pregnant women may experience bleeding gums.
- Some women notice bleeding while brushing their teeth.
- The author says that these problems do not always require special treatment.
- Increased saliva production may also occur during pregnancy.
- In some cases, women may feel nauseated because of excessive saliva.
- The author recommends maintaining good oral hygiene.
5. Excessive saliva and nausea
- Some women produce more saliva than usual during pregnancy.
- This can become uncomfortable, especially when combined with nausea.
- Swallowing large amounts of saliva may make nausea worse.
- The author explains that some women prefer spitting out excess saliva.
- The condition generally improves as pregnancy progresses.
6. Varicose veins during pregnancy
- Varicose veins occur when veins become enlarged and swollen.
- During pregnancy, the growing uterus can put pressure on blood vessels.
- This may slow the return of blood from the legs.
- Varicose veins can appear in the legs and sometimes around the genital area.
- The author explains that pregnancy hormones may also contribute to this problem.
7. Why varicose veins develop
- Veins contain valves that help blood flow toward the heart.
- During pregnancy, increased pressure may make these valves less effective.
- Blood can collect in the veins, causing them to become enlarged.
- The author says that varicose veins are common during the later stages of pregnancy.
- Some women may notice swollen or twisted veins.
8. Swelling of the legs
- Swelling is common during pregnancy, especially in the later months.
- The author mentions that approximately 40% of pregnant women may experience swelling.
- Swelling occurs when fluid collects in the body's tissues.
- The legs and feet are commonly affected.
- Swelling may become more noticeable after standing for a long time.
9. How to reduce swelling
- The author recommends resting with the legs raised.
- Elevating the legs may help improve blood circulation.
- Pregnant women should avoid standing for excessively long periods.
- The author suggests monitoring swelling and body weight.
- If swelling becomes severe, medical advice should be sought.
10. Sudden weight gain
- The author warns that sudden weight gain may indicate abnormal fluid retention.
- According to the book, gaining more than 500 grams in one week may be a warning sign.
- The author recommends consulting a doctor if this happens.
- However, normal pregnancy weight gain varies from person to person.
11. Numbness and tingling in the hands
- Some pregnant women experience numbness or tingling in their fingers.
- The author explains that swelling may put pressure on nerves.
- This can cause discomfort in the hands.
- Some women may experience pain or difficulty moving their fingers.
- These symptoms may become more noticeable during the later stages of pregnancy.
12. Heartburn during pregnancy
- Heartburn is another common pregnancy problem.
- As the uterus grows, it can put pressure on the stomach.
- Stomach acid may move upward into the esophagus.
- This causes a burning sensation in the chest.
- The author recommends eating smaller meals to reduce discomfort.
13. Lower back pain
- Back pain is common during the second half of pregnancy.
- As the baby grows, the mother's center of gravity changes.
- The muscles around the lower back may become tired.
- This can cause discomfort and stiffness.
- The author recommends avoiding unnecessary strain.
- Pregnant women should maintain comfortable posture and rest when needed.
14. Skin pigmentation changes
- Pregnancy can cause certain areas of the skin to become darker.
- The author mentions darkening around the nipples.
- Similar changes may appear on the face and genital area.
- These changes are caused by hormonal activity.
- The author explains that such pigmentation changes are generally normal during pregnancy.

Main message of this page
The author explains several common problems during pregnancy, including constipation, insomnia, nosebleeds, bleeding gums, varicose veins, swelling, numbness, heartburn, back pain, and skin pigmentation changes.

Most of these symptoms are common and may improve after childbirth, but some require medical attention.

Important medical context: Sudden swelling, particularly of the face or hands, severe headaches, visual changes, or rapidly increasing weight can be warning signs of preeclampsia and require prompt medical assessment. A fixed weight-gain threshold of 500 grams per week is not appropriate for every pregnancy.
