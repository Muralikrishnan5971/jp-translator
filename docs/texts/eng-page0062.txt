Chapter 24. The Baby's Bed
1. Does a baby need a separate bed?
- The author begins by discussing whether a newborn baby needs a separate bed.
- The book mentions that some families may not have enough space for a dedicated baby bed.
- In small homes, arranging a separate sleeping area can be difficult.
- The author recognizes that families have different living conditions.
2. Living in a small house
- The author gives an example of a family living in a small Japanese home.
- Such homes may have only one or two rooms.
- Parents may find it difficult to make space for a baby's bed.
- The author suggests that sleeping arrangements should take the family's available space into account.
3. A baby needs a safe sleeping place
- Every family should provide a safe sleeping space for their baby.
- The most important reason for using a baby bed is to protect the baby.
- A baby should not accidentally fall from the bed.
- As babies grow, they begin moving more and may fall if the bed is not properly protected.
4. The importance of bed rails
- The book recommends using a baby bed with protective rails.
- These rails help prevent the baby from falling.
- The rails should be high enough to protect the baby.
- However, the author warns that simply having high rails is not enough.
- The design and spacing of the rails are also important.
5. Choosing the correct spacing between rails
- The author recommends placing the vertical bars of the bed close together.
- The bars should be spaced so that the baby's head cannot pass between them.
- If the gaps are too wide, the baby's head may become trapped.
- This can cause serious injury.
- The author also warns about the possibility of the baby's neck getting caught between the bars.
6. Avoiding dangerous bed designs
- The author explains that some bed designs may look attractive but can be dangerous.
- A bed with poorly designed rails may allow the baby's body to slip through.
- If the baby's head becomes trapped, the baby may be unable to breathe.
- Parents should therefore pay attention to safety rather than appearance.
7. The height of the baby's bed
- The author discusses whether a baby bed should be high or low.
- A low bed may make it difficult for parents to bend down and pick up the baby.
- However, a high bed can be dangerous if the baby falls.
- The author suggests choosing a bed that is convenient for parents while remaining safe for the baby.
8. Protecting the baby's head
- Babies may move their heads and bodies while sleeping.
- The author recommends preventing the baby's head from hitting hard parts of the bed.
- The book discusses using protective materials around certain parts of the bed.
- The aim is to prevent injuries when the baby moves.
9. Cleaning and disinfecting the bed
- The author explains that the baby's bed should be kept clean.
- If the bed is second-hand or has been stored for a long time, it should be cleaned before use.
- The book mentions disinfecting the bed with a weak disinfectant solution.
- It describes using a solution containing approximately 2% disinfectant.
- The author also recommends wiping the bed thoroughly and allowing it to dry.
10. Choosing bedding materials
- The author discusses the importance of selecting suitable bedding.
- The baby's bedding should be comfortable and easy to clean.
- Parents should avoid materials that may cause irritation.
- The bed should be prepared before the baby is brought home.
11. Choosing safe bed materials
- The author warns that some painted beds may contain harmful substances.
- Older paints sometimes contained lead.
- Lead is poisonous, especially to young children.
- Babies may bite or chew the rails of their beds.
- If the paint contains lead, the baby may swallow small amounts.
- The author therefore recommends avoiding beds painted with lead-containing paint.
12. Babies may chew their beds
- As babies grow, they begin exploring objects with their mouths.
- They may bite the wooden or metal parts of their beds.
- The author warns that paint can peel off when babies chew the rails.
- Swallowing paint containing lead can cause lead poisoning.
- Parents should choose beds made with safe materials.
13. Using mosquito nets
- The book mentions baby beds fitted with mosquito nets.
- A mosquito net can help protect a baby from insect bites.
- However, the author warns that nets can become dangerous as babies grow.
- A baby may become tangled in the net.
- Parents should therefore be careful when using mosquito nets around the bed.
The discussion about mosquito nets continues on the next page.

The author emphasizes that a baby's bed should be chosen mainly for safety, not appearance or convenience.

The important points are:
1. Use a safe sleeping space that prevents falls.
2. Make sure the bed rails are properly designed.
3. Avoid large gaps where the baby's head or neck could become trapped.
4. Choose a bed of suitable height.
5. Keep the bed clean.
6. Avoid beds containing lead-based paint.
7. Be careful with mosquito nets and other materials that could become tangled around the baby.

The author explains that a newborn baby needs a clean, comfortable, and safe environment, but parents do not necessarily need an expensive or specially designed room.

The main ideas are:
1. Choose a clean and comfortable room for the baby.
2. Maintain a suitable temperature and humidity.
3. Avoid excessive heating or cooling.
4. Use heating equipment safely.
5. Keep insects, rats, and other pests away.
6. Prepare the baby's room before childbirth.
7. Consider the available space when choosing a baby's bed.
Note: The temperature examples and heating practices come from an older Japanese childcare book. Modern infant-care guidance emphasizes avoiding overheating, maintaining safe ventilation, and using a separate, firm, flat infant sleep surface.
