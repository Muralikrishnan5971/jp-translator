Chapter 21. The Role of the Father (父親の役割)
1. Childbirth used to involve the whole family
- In the past, childbirth was an important event for the entire extended family.
- Women usually gave birth at home, with help from family members.
- In modern times, many families have become smaller nuclear families, consisting of parents and their children.
- Because of this change, childbirth has become more of a private responsibility for the husband and wife.
2. Husbands should take responsibility
- The author says that fathers should actively help their wives during pregnancy and childbirth.
- When a pregnant woman is admitted to a hospital, her husband may have to manage household responsibilities.
- Even if household work is difficult, the husband should make an effort.
- Men should learn to perform simple household tasks themselves.
- For example, washing clothes or cleaning the house should not be considered work that only women can do.
3. Pregnancy is not a reason to stop helping at home
- Some husbands expect their pregnant wives to continue doing all the household work.
- The author criticizes this attitude.
- A husband should understand that pregnancy can make daily activities more difficult.
- He should help his wife rather than leaving everything to her.
4. Husbands should prepare for childbirth
- Some women give birth earlier than expected.
- A husband should know what to do if labor begins unexpectedly.
- The book mentions that childbirth can happen around the 9th month (32 weeks) of pregnancy.
- Husbands should be prepared to arrange transportation and medical assistance.
- The author also mentions that some maternity facilities may not accept women unless arrangements have been made beforehand.
5. Fathers should stop smoking around children
- The author strongly advises fathers to avoid smoking around pregnant women and children.
- Smoking inside the home exposes family members to harmful tobacco smoke.
- The book gives an example of a father who smoked heavily.
- His young child developed breathing problems, including bronchitis and asthma-like symptoms.
- The author suggests that the father's smoking may have contributed to these problems.
6. Smoking can harm children's health
- The author explains that children who live with smokers may experience respiratory problems.
- Tobacco smoke can affect the lungs and airways.
- The book mentions that children living in smoking households may be smaller or less healthy than other children.
- Parents should avoid exposing babies and children to tobacco smoke.
7. Fathers should set a good example
- Parents have a responsibility to create a healthy environment for their children.
- Fathers should not smoke around babies or pregnant women.
- The author encourages fathers to think about how their habits affect their families.
- A father's responsibilities begin before the baby is born.

Chapter 22. Should You Have Children or Not? (子どもをもつか、もたぬか)
8. Deciding whether to have children
- The next section begins by discussing whether a couple should have children.
- The author suggests that raising children requires responsibility and preparation.
- Having a child is an important decision that affects both parents.
9. Childcare and family planning
- The author mentions that some readers may think the discussion of childcare arrangements is unusual.
- However, the book explains that childcare is not simply a matter of personal preference.
- Parents should think carefully about their ability and willingness to raise children.

Main message of Page 27
The author emphasizes that a father should actively participate in pregnancy, childbirth, household work, and childcare.

The main lessons are:
1. Childbirth is a shared responsibility between husband and wife.
2. Husbands should help with household work during pregnancy.
3. Fathers should prepare for unexpected childbirth situations.
4. Smoking around pregnant women and children should be avoided.
5. Parents should think carefully before deciding to have children.
Note: This translation simplifies the original Japanese and preserves the author's general meaning. Some statements reflect the medical knowledge and social attitudes of the time when the book was written.
