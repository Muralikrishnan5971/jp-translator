1. Pregnancy complications in older women
- Women aged 35 or older who are giving birth for the first time may face certain additional pregnancy risks.

2. Preeclampsia (Pregnancy-related high blood pressure)
- The author discusses a condition traditionally called pregnancy toxemia, now commonly associated with preeclampsia.
- This condition may cause high blood pressure during pregnancy.
- Protein may appear in the urine.
- Swelling of the body may also occur.
- The author explains that these symptoms may indicate a serious pregnancy complication.
3. Detecting preeclampsia
- The author recommends regular medical examinations during pregnancy.
- Doctors should check blood pressure and urine.
- The text says that swelling alone does not necessarily indicate preeclampsia.
- However, sudden or excessive swelling may require medical attention.
- The author mentions that gaining more than 500 grams in one week during late pregnancy should prompt a medical check.
4. Treating preeclampsia
- The author says that preeclampsia should be treated as early as possible.
- In the past, women were sometimes advised to remain at home and rest.
- However, the author argues that hospital treatment may be necessary.
- The condition can become dangerous if blood pressure rises significantly.
- The author recommends hospital care when the condition becomes serious.
5. Severe preeclampsia and childbirth
- The author explains that severe preeclampsia can threaten the lives of both mother and baby.
- In some cases, doctors may need to deliver the baby earlier than expected.
- The author discusses medical decisions involving premature delivery.
- The timing of delivery depends on the severity of the condition.
- The author emphasizes that doctors must carefully consider the risks to both mother and child.
6. Placental abruption
- The author discusses placental abruption, a condition in which the placenta separates from the uterus before childbirth.
- This can cause serious bleeding.
- The baby may receive less oxygen and nutrients.
- The mother may also lose a dangerous amount of blood.
- Placental abruption requires urgent medical attention.
7. Treatment for placental abruption
- The author explains that significant placental separation may require emergency delivery.
- A cesarean section may sometimes be necessary.
- The text discusses the importance of controlling bleeding.
- Blood transfusions may be needed when blood loss is severe.
- The author recommends urgent hospital treatment.
8. Preparing for blood transfusions
- The author mentions that blood transfusions can be important during serious pregnancy complications.
- Certain conditions may cause major blood loss.
- The author discusses the importance of having compatible blood available.
- Hospitals should be prepared to provide emergency treatment.
- Blood type compatibility must be checked before transfusion.

Chapter 13: Infectious Diseases During Pregnancy
9. Infections and fetal development
- The author explains that some infections during pregnancy can affect the developing baby.
- Certain viruses can pass from the mother to the fetus.
- These infections may cause birth defects or other complications.
- The author emphasizes that preventing infections during pregnancy is important.
10. Rubella infection during pregnancy
- Rubella, also known as German measles, is a viral infection.
- The author explains that rubella infection during early pregnancy can be dangerous.
- The virus may interfere with the development of the baby's organs.
- Possible abnormalities mentioned include problems affecting:
  - The eyes.
  - The ears.
  - The heart.
- The author warns that infection during the first few months is particularly concerning.
11. Rubella and congenital abnormalities
- The author explains that rubella infection can sometimes cause congenital rubella syndrome.
- This condition may affect several parts of the baby's body.
- The book states that infection during the first five months of pregnancy may carry risks.
- However, the risk is especially high during early pregnancy.
- The author emphasizes the importance of rubella prevention.
12. Rubella vaccination before pregnancy
- The author recommends checking rubella immunity before becoming pregnant.
- Women who are not immune should consider vaccination before pregnancy.
- The book discusses the rubella vaccine as a preventive measure.
- The author mentions that vaccination should be completed before conception.
- The text advises avoiding pregnancy for a period after vaccination.
13. Rubella antibody testing
- A blood test can determine whether a woman has antibodies against rubella.
- The author recommends checking antibody levels before pregnancy.
- Women who have previously received vaccination may already be immune.
- Some women may have immunity because of a previous infection.
- The author emphasizes that immunity testing can help women prepare for pregnancy.
14. Importance of preventing infection
- The author says that preventing infection is safer than trying to treat its effects after the baby has been exposed.
- Pregnant women should be aware of infections that can affect fetal development.
- The author encourages medical consultation when infection is suspected.

The first part discusses serious pregnancy complications, particularly preeclampsia and placental abruption. These conditions may require urgent hospital treatment.

The second part introduces infectious diseases during pregnancy, focusing on rubella and its potential effects on the developing baby.
