Chapter 27: Baby Bottles (ミルクびん)
1. How many diapers should you prepare?
1. The book recommends preparing about 25–30 cloth diapers.
2. Babies urinate frequently, so many diapers are needed.
3. If the mother has difficulty doing laundry or the household has special circumstances, more diapers may be necessary.
4. The author suggests using disposable diapers in such situations.

2. Should you buy a baby bathtub?
1. Baby bathtubs are sold in shops, but the author says they are not always necessary.
2. In older Japanese homes, families often used large wooden bathing tubs.
3. A baby could be bathed using a suitable tub already available at home.
4. The author explains that buying a separate bathtub may be unnecessary.
5. Parents should consider their household facilities before purchasing one.

3. Be careful when bathing babies
1. The author warns against using dangerously hot water.
2. Babies have sensitive skin and can be burned easily.
3. A baby should not be placed in a bathtub containing very hot water.
4. Parents should ensure the water temperature is suitable before bathing the baby.

4. Why baby bottles may be needed
1. The author strongly believes that breastfeeding is the best way to feed a baby.
2. However, some mothers may have difficulty breastfeeding.
3. In such situations, baby bottles may be necessary.
4. The book recommends preparing bottles in advance in case they are needed.
5. Bottles may also be used to give babies water or fruit juice, according to the book's older feeding practices.

5. Choosing a baby bottle
1. The author recommends using a glass baby bottle.
2. A glass bottle with a capacity of approximately 200 mL is suggested.
3. Glass bottles are easier to clean thoroughly.
4. Glass can be sterilized using boiling water.
5. The author points out that glass bottles do not easily change their shape when exposed to hot water.

6. Problems with plastic baby bottles
1. Plastic baby bottles were also available when the book was written.
2. The author mentions that plastic can become cloudy after repeated use.
3. Some plastic bottles become less transparent, making it difficult to see whether they are clean.
4. The book also mentions plastic bottles with capacities of approximately 240–300 mL.
5. The author prefers glass bottles because they are easier to inspect and clean.

7. Avoid buying unnecessarily large bottles
1. The author says that a baby's milk intake is relatively small during the early months.
2. A bottle with a capacity of about 200 mL is generally sufficient initially.
3. The book suggests that very large bottles are unnecessary for newborns.
4. Parents should choose a bottle based on the baby's feeding requirements.

8. Baby bottles with handles
1. Some bottles are designed with handles.
2. These handles allow older babies to hold the bottle themselves.
3. The author mentions that babies eventually learn to grasp objects and feed themselves.
4. However, newborn babies cannot hold their own bottles.
5. The author suggests that handled bottles are not essential during the newborn period.

9. Choosing the bottle nipple
1. Baby bottle nipples are available in different shapes and materials.
2. The book mentions nipples made from silicone rubber.
3. Some nipples have smaller holes, while others have larger holes.
4. The size of the hole affects how quickly milk flows.
5. Nipples are sold in different sizes, including small (S), medium (M), and large (L).
6. Parents should choose an appropriate nipple for their baby's feeding needs.

10. Rubber versus silicone nipples
1. Traditional bottle nipples were often made from rubber.
2. Silicone nipples became another available option.
3. The author discusses the differences between these materials.
4. Some nipples are softer, while others are firmer.
5. The important thing is that the baby can drink comfortably.

11. How many bottles should you buy?
1. The author advises parents not to buy too many bottles before the baby is born.
2. Some babies are breastfed and may not need bottles regularly.
3. Parents can purchase additional bottles later if necessary.
4. The author recommends keeping the packaging and instructions until the product has been used.
5. Parents should avoid buying unnecessary baby products simply because they are available.

12. Preparing baby bottles before birth
1. Parents often purchase baby supplies before delivery.
2. The author advises buying only essential items initially.
3. Additional feeding equipment can be purchased after the baby's needs become clear.
4. The book suggests that preparing everything far in advance is not always necessary.

The author emphasizes that parents should prepare essential feeding equipment without buying unnecessary products.

The main recommendations are:
1. Breastfeeding is encouraged.
2. Keep baby bottles available if needed.
3. Glass bottles are recommended by the author.
4. A 200 mL bottle is suggested for early feeding.
5. Choose nipples with an appropriate milk flow.
6. Avoid purchasing too many bottles before birth.
7. Select baby products based on actual needs rather than appearance.
Modern medical note: Some recommendations in this older book are outdated. Current guidance recommends exclusive breastfeeding for approximately the first six months when possible, or appropriate infant formula when needed. Babies younger than six months generally should not be given water or fruit juice unless specifically directed by a healthcare professional. Glass and modern food-grade plastic bottles can both be suitable when properly maintained and cleaned.
