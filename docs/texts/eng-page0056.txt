Chapter 16: Things You Should Not Do During Pregnancy
1. Medicines can affect the unborn baby
- Medicines taken by a pregnant woman can sometimes affect her baby.
- The first three months of pregnancy are especially important because the baby's body is developing.
- Some medicines can interfere with the baby's nervous system.
- The author warns that medicines affecting nerve function may be particularly dangerous during this period.
2. Be careful with medicines during early pregnancy
- Some women may take medicines before realizing they are pregnant.
- The author gives the example of medicines that affect the nervous system.
- These medicines may cause problems with the baby's development.
- Pregnant women should therefore be careful about taking medicines without medical advice.
3. Headaches and shoulder stiffness during pregnancy
- Some pregnant women experience headaches, dizziness, and shoulder stiffness.
- The author explains that these symptoms may be related to pregnancy.
- However, headaches and shoulder stiffness do not always mean that a woman is pregnant.
- Doctors should consider the possibility of pregnancy before prescribing medicines.
4. Medicines for headaches and muscle relaxation
- The author mentions several types of medicines:
  - Painkillers for headaches.
  - Tranquilizers or sedatives.
  - Muscle relaxants for shoulder stiffness.
- Some of these medicines may not have established safety during pregnancy.
- The author advises caution when using them.
5. Doctors must consider the benefits and risks
- The author explains that doctors sometimes prescribe medicines even when their safety during pregnancy has not been fully established.
- Such medicines should be used only when the expected benefits are greater than the possible risks.
- Doctors must carefully consider the woman's condition before prescribing treatment.
6. Women should inform doctors about possible pregnancy
- Women who might be pregnant should tell their doctors before receiving medicines.
- The author warns that pregnancy can sometimes go unnoticed during its early stages.
- A doctor may prescribe medicine without knowing that the woman is pregnant.
- Therefore, women should mention any possibility of pregnancy when seeking treatment.
7. Pregnancy testing
- The author mentions that doctors may not always immediately recognize a pregnancy.
- Women who suspect pregnancy should seek medical confirmation.
- Early confirmation can help doctors choose appropriate treatments.
8. Other precautions during pregnancy
- The text also discusses the importance of avoiding unnecessary risks during pregnancy.
- Women should seek medical advice when they experience unusual symptoms.
- Proper medical care can help protect both the mother and the developing baby.

Chapter 17: Returning Home for Childbirth
9. What is Satogaeri Bunben?
- In Japan, some pregnant women return to their parents' home to give birth.
- This tradition is called Satogaeri Bunben (里帰り分娩).
- It allows women to receive help from their parents and family members during childbirth.
- The author explains that this practice is common among women who live far away from their hometowns.
10. Why women return to their parents' home
- Some young couples move from rural areas to large cities.
- They get married and establish their own households.
- When the wife becomes pregnant, she may decide to return to her hometown for childbirth.
- This allows her to receive support from her family.
11. Choosing a hospital for childbirth
- A woman who plans to give birth in her hometown needs to find a maternity hospital there.
- She may have received prenatal care from a different hospital during the early stages of pregnancy.
- Therefore, she must arrange to receive care at the hospital where she plans to deliver.
- The author discusses the importance of coordinating care between hospitals.
12. Problems with changing hospitals
- Some women may face difficulties when transferring to another maternity hospital.
- Hospitals may have their own rules for accepting pregnant women.
- The author mentions that certain hospitals may require women to attend their prenatal care services before childbirth.
- Women should therefore plan their hospital arrangements early.
13. Support during childbirth
- The author explains that childbirth is an important family event.
- Parents and relatives can provide emotional and practical support.
- Returning to the family home can make childbirth preparations easier.
- However, the author also discusses the responsibilities of the husband.
14. The husband's responsibilities
- The author suggests that husbands should participate in preparing for childbirth.
- Some husbands may leave most responsibilities to their wives or their wives' parents.
- The author argues that husbands should not avoid their responsibilities.
- Childbirth and raising a child should involve both parents.
15. Becoming a father
- The author explains that helping during pregnancy and childbirth can help a husband understand his responsibilities as a father.
- Supporting his wife can strengthen the relationship between the couple.
- The author suggests that fathers should take an active role in family life rather than relying entirely on relatives.

Main message of Page 23
Chapter 16: Medicines taken during pregnancy can sometimes affect the developing baby. Women should tell doctors if they are pregnant or might be pregnant, and treatment decisions should consider both benefits and risks.

Chapter 17: Returning to one's parents' home for childbirth is a Japanese tradition that provides family support. However, the author emphasizes that husbands should also take responsibility for childbirth preparations and raising their children.

Medical note: The book's discussion reflects its time of publication. Many medicines can be used safely during pregnancy, and necessary prescribed medicines should not be stopped without consulting a healthcare professional.
