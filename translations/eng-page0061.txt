Chapter 22: Should You Have Children or Not? (Continued)
1. Raising children requires effort
- Raising children requires physical strength and energy.
- The author says it is generally easier for younger parents to raise children.
- When both husband and wife are young, they may have more energy to manage childcare.
- However, marriage and having children can affect a woman's ability to continue working.
- Some women leave their jobs after marriage or pregnancy.
- Others continue working but may become so busy that they neglect their health.
2. Pregnancy and childbirth require support
- The author explains that pregnancy and childbirth can be physically demanding.
- A woman who has recently given birth needs help and support.
- If the husband is also busy working, the mother may have to manage childcare alone.
- The author believes that parents should think carefully about whether they can provide enough care for their children.
- Couples should consider their family circumstances before deciding to have children.
3. Having children is a personal decision
- The author says that couples can decide whether they want to have children.
- If they do not want children, they can use contraception to prevent pregnancy.
- The book encourages couples who do not want children to take precautions rather than relying on chance.
- The author also suggests that couples who want children should consider having them relatively early.
4. Methods of preventing pregnancy
- The book discusses different contraceptive methods.
- In the past, people relied on identifying the days when pregnancy was less likely.
- However, the author explains that these methods are not always reliable.
- Modern contraceptive methods offer other ways to prevent pregnancy.
5. The contraceptive pill
- The author explains that the contraceptive pill contains female hormones.
- These hormones include estrogen and progestogen.
- The pill works mainly by preventing ovulation, meaning the release of an egg from the ovary.
- The book describes the pill as an effective method of contraception.
- However, it also discusses possible side effects and medical concerns.
6. How the pill is taken
The book describes an older 21-day pill schedule:
1. Start taking the pill on the fifth day after menstruation begins.
2. Take one pill every day for 21 days.
3. Stop taking the pill for seven days.
4. Menstrual-like bleeding usually occurs during this break.
5. Start the next course after the seven-day break, even if bleeding has not completely stopped.
The author warns that missing pills can reduce contraceptive protection.
7. What happens if a pill is forgotten?
- The book advises taking the forgotten pill as soon as possible.
- If one pill is missed, it says to take it when remembered.
- If two pills are missed, the book describes taking additional pills according to its schedule.
- The author explains that irregular pill use can cause unexpected bleeding.
- Missing pills may also reduce the effectiveness of contraception.
8. Possible side effects of contraceptive pills
- The author discusses several possible side effects.
- Some women may experience nausea or vomiting.
- Others may have changes in menstrual bleeding.
- The book mentions that contraceptive pills may affect blood clotting.
- It warns that women with high blood pressure should be particularly careful.
- Smoking while using certain hormonal contraceptives can increase health risks.
9. Medical precautions
- The author advises women aged 35 or older who smoke to avoid certain contraceptive pills.
- Women with high blood pressure, liver problems, or other medical conditions may need special precautions.
- The book recommends medical supervision when using hormonal contraception.
- It also mentions that contraceptive pills may be unsuitable for some women.
10. Intrauterine devices (IUDs)
- The book introduces the IUD as another method of contraception.
- An IUD is a small device placed inside the uterus.
- The author describes older IUDs made from metal rings or loops.
- These devices were intended to prevent pregnancy without requiring daily medication.
- The book explains that an IUD must be inserted by a healthcare professional.
11. Possible problems with IUDs
- Some women may experience bleeding after an IUD is inserted.
- The book mentions that the device can sometimes cause discomfort or unexpected bleeding.
- The author explains that certain women may not be suitable candidates for this method.
- It also discusses the possibility of pregnancy occurring despite an IUD.
12. Ectopic pregnancy
- The author discusses ectopic pregnancy, in which a fertilized egg develops outside the uterus.
- This can occur in the fallopian tube.
- Ectopic pregnancy is a serious medical condition.
- The book explains that abnormal bleeding may be one of its symptoms.
- Severe abdominal pain and bleeding require medical attention.

Main message of Page 28
The author emphasizes that having children should be a carefully considered decision, and couples who wish to avoid pregnancy should understand the available contraceptive methods.

The main ideas are:
1. Raising children requires time, energy, and responsibility.
2. Couples should decide together whether and when to have children.
3. Contraceptive pills can prevent pregnancy but may have side effects.
4. Missing contraceptive pills can reduce their effectiveness.
5. IUDs provide another method of contraception.
6. Women should consider their health conditions when choosing contraception.
Important note: This is a simplified translation of an older Japanese medical book, not current medical guidance. Modern contraceptive pills have different formulations and missed-pill instructions. The schedule described in the book should not be followed without checking the instructions for the specific medication and consulting a healthcare professional.
