Chapter 6: Care Before Pregnancy (Continued)
1. Rubella vaccination before pregnancy
- Women should avoid becoming pregnant for one month before vaccination and two months after vaccination, according to the book.
- A woman may test positive for rubella antibodies even if she has never been vaccinated.
- This may mean she already has immunity from an earlier infection.
- However, if she becomes infected with rubella during pregnancy, the baby may be affected.
- If a blood test shows antibodies called IgM, it may indicate a recent infection.
- The author advises waiting and repeating the test after one or two weeks.
- If the antibody level increases fourfold or more, the author recommends considering termination of the pregnancy.
2. Toxoplasmosis and pregnancy
- The author discusses toxoplasmosis, an infection caused by a parasite.
- Toxoplasmosis can be dangerous during pregnancy.
- If a woman becomes infected before pregnancy, the risk to the baby may be different from infection acquired during pregnancy.
- The author warns that women should be careful about infections before becoming pregnant.
3. Testing for hepatitis B
- The author recommends testing for hepatitis B before pregnancy.
- Hepatitis B is a viral infection that affects the liver.
- The book states that approximately 20–40% of people may have evidence of previous hepatitis B exposure.
- The author mentions a blood marker called HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen).
- A positive HBsAg test indicates a current hepatitis B infection.
- The author recommends further testing if the result is positive.
- The text also mentions another hepatitis B marker, HBeAg.
- The author explains that these tests can help doctors understand hepatitis B infection and its possible risks.
4. Other health checks before pregnancy
The author recommends checking for existing health problems before becoming pregnant.
- High blood pressure: Women should have their blood pressure checked.
- Anemia: Low hemoglobin levels should be identified and treated.
- Diabetes: Blood sugar levels should be checked and controlled.
- Nutritional problems: Poor nutrition should be corrected.
- Alcohol and tobacco: The author recommends avoiding both.
- General health: Existing illnesses should be managed before pregnancy.
5. Importance of planning pregnancy
- The author says that pregnancy preparation is particularly important for newly married couples.
- Couples should learn about pregnancy and childbirth before trying to conceive.
- The author criticizes the lack of proper pregnancy education.
- Many people enter marriage without sufficient knowledge about pregnancy.
- The author believes that preparation can reduce unnecessary risks.
6. Controlling blood sugar during pregnancy
- The author warns that poorly controlled diabetes can cause complications during pregnancy.
- Blood sugar levels should be carefully monitored.
- The text emphasizes that pregnant women with diabetes need proper medical supervision.
- Keeping blood sugar within the recommended range is important for the health of both mother and baby.
7. Fear of childbirth
- The author explains that women giving birth for the first time may feel anxious.
- Fear of labor pain is common.
- Some women worry about whether they will be able to tolerate childbirth.
- The author discusses methods that may help women feel more comfortable during labor.
8. Lamaze method of childbirth
- The author introduces the Lamaze method, a childbirth preparation technique.
- It focuses on helping women cope with labor pain.
- It involves breathing techniques, relaxation, and mental preparation.
- The author describes it as a form of psychological preparation for childbirth.
- The method teaches women how to relax muscles that are not needed during labor.
- The author says that understanding childbirth may reduce fear and anxiety.
- The method does not necessarily eliminate pain completely, but it may make labor easier to manage.

Main message of this page
The author emphasizes that women should prepare for pregnancy by checking their immunity to infections, testing for hepatitis B, controlling existing medical conditions, avoiding alcohol and tobacco, and learning about childbirth.

Important medical context: This is an older medical book, and some recommendations are outdated. Current guidance generally recommends avoiding pregnancy for 28 days after rubella/MMR vaccination, rather than two months. A positive rubella IgM result alone is not a reason to terminate a pregnancy; it requires careful interpretation and confirmatory testing. Likewise, hepatitis B prevalence figures in the book should not be treated as current estimates
