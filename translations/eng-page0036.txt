Chapter 4: Hereditary Diseases
Introduction to hereditary diseases
- The next chapter begins by discussing diseases that may be inherited from parents.
- These diseases are connected to genetic factors.
- The author mentions examples such as:
  - Phenylketonuria (PKU), a metabolic disorder.
  - Skin diseases.
  - Muscle diseases, including muscular dystrophy.
- The chapter begins explaining how inherited diseases may affect human health.

1. Medicines and pregnancy
- Some medicines taken during pregnancy may affect the developing baby.
- The author warns that pregnant women should avoid taking unnecessary medicines.
- Certain medicines may cause abnormalities in babies or complications during pregnancy.
- The author argues that medicines should not be considered safe simply because they are available in pharmacies.
2. Antihistamines and birth defects
- Antihistamines are medicines commonly used to treat allergies.
- The author mentions reports warning about their use during pregnancy.
- According to the text, some antihistamines were suspected of causing birth defects.
- Pregnant women are advised by the author to avoid unnecessary medication.
3. Hormonal medicines and pregnancy
- Synthetic hormones can affect the body's natural processes.
- The author discusses synthetic progesterone-like hormones used to prevent miscarriage.
- A medicine called Salidomide (as written in the text) is mentioned in connection with synthetic hormones and birth defects.
- The author claims that some synthetic hormones may interfere with normal fetal development.
- Reports from other countries suggested abnormalities in the reproductive organs of babies exposed to certain hormones.
- The author questions whether medicines intended to prevent miscarriage are always safe.
4. Medicines used to prevent miscarriage
- The author states that there is no completely reliable medicine for preventing miscarriage.
- Synthetic hormones were sometimes prescribed to women at risk of miscarriage.
- However, the author argues that these medicines may not always be necessary.
- The author questions the safety of using synthetic hormones during pregnancy.
5. Anesthesia during childbirth
- Anesthesia is used to reduce pain during surgery and childbirth.
- The author discusses medicines used during childbirth, including epidural anesthesia.
- Epidural anesthesia involves injecting medicine near the spinal nerves.
- The author warns that anesthesia can sometimes cause complications.
- Possible complications mentioned include:
  - Problems with the nervous system.
  - Heart-related problems.
  - Joint problems.
  - Other medical complications.
- The author says that anesthetic medicines should be administered carefully by trained medical professionals.
6. Alcohol and birth defects
- The author discusses the relationship between alcohol consumption during pregnancy and birth defects.
- The text claims that drinking alcohol during pregnancy may increase the risk of abnormalities in babies.
- It gives the following figures:
Alcohol consumption	Claimed birth defect rate
5–10 drinks per day	1 in 5 babies
25 or more drinks per day	1 in 10 babies
- These are historical claims from the book and should not be treated as reliable modern statistics.
- The author emphasizes that alcohol consumption during pregnancy can be dangerous.
7. Alcohol during early pregnancy
- The author states that alcohol exposure may be especially dangerous around conception and during early pregnancy.
- Alcohol may affect the baby's development before the mother even realizes she is pregnant.
- The author suggests that avoiding alcohol during pregnancy is the safest approach.
8. Other medicines linked to birth defects
- The author mentions several medicines that were suspected of causing abnormalities in babies.
- One example is LSD, a hallucinogenic drug.
- Another is progesterone, a hormone involved in pregnancy.
- The author also mentions methotrexate, a medicine that affects cell growth.
- The author warns that these substances may interfere with normal fetal development.
