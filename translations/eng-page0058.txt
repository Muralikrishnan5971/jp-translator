Chapter 18: When Should You Be Admitted to the Hospital? (Continued)
1. Signs that labor is beginning
- When labor starts, a woman may experience abdominal pain and contractions.
- She may also notice changes in vaginal discharge.
- Some women experience a small amount of blood mixed with mucus.
- This is commonly called the bloody show, which can be a sign that labor is approaching.
2. Water breaking during pregnancy
- Another sign of labor is when the amniotic sac breaks and fluid begins leaking.
- This is commonly called water breaking.
- After the water breaks, abdominal contractions may become regular.
- The author explains that contractions may gradually become stronger.
3. When should a woman go to the hospital?
- The author describes contractions occurring about every 10 minutes as an important sign.
- When contractions become regular and close together, the woman should contact the hospital.
- Some women may need to go to the hospital earlier, depending on their condition.
- The author emphasizes that the timing of hospital admission can vary.
4. First-time mothers and experienced mothers
- Labor can progress differently for women having their first baby and women who have given birth before.
- The author says that first-time mothers may have a longer labor.
- Women who have previously given birth may experience faster labor.
- Therefore, women who have given birth before may need to leave for the hospital sooner.

Chapter 19: The Baby Is Not Born Even After the Due Date
Japanese title: 予定日になっても生まれない
5. The expected delivery date is only an estimate
- When pregnancy is confirmed, doctors calculate an expected delivery date.
- However, the author explains that this date is not completely accurate.
- It is impossible to predict the exact day when every baby will be born.
- Some babies are born before the expected date.
- Others are born after it.
6. Giving birth before the expected date
- The author says that a baby may be born two or three weeks before the expected delivery date.
- This does not necessarily mean something is wrong.
- The expected date is simply a guide for estimating when childbirth may happen.
7. Giving birth after the expected date
- Some women do not experience labor even after their expected delivery date.
- The author says that women who pass their due date may begin worrying.
- However, passing the expected date does not automatically mean there is a problem.
- Doctors need to assess the mother's and baby's condition.
8. Monitoring the baby after the due date
- The author explains that doctors can examine the baby and placenta when pregnancy continues beyond the expected date.
- They may check the placenta's function and the baby's condition.
- These examinations help doctors decide whether waiting is appropriate.
9. Problems with continuing pregnancy too long
- The author discusses the possibility that the placenta may not function as well when pregnancy continues for too long.
- If the placenta cannot adequately support the baby, complications may occur.
- Therefore, doctors should monitor pregnancies that continue beyond the expected delivery date.
10. Inducing labor artificially
- The author explains that some doctors use medicines or medical procedures to start labor.
- This is called labor induction.
- The decision depends on the condition of the mother and baby.
- The author discusses how medical practices have changed over time.
11. Planned childbirth
- The author introduces the idea of planned delivery (計画分娩).
- In this method, doctors arrange childbirth at a particular time instead of waiting for labor to begin naturally.
- The author says this approach has sometimes been used for medical and practical reasons.
12. Artificially starting contractions
- The author explains that doctors can use medicines to encourage uterine contractions.
- This may help start the process of childbirth.
- However, the author also discusses the importance of checking whether the cervix is ready for labor.
- The cervix needs to soften and open for vaginal delivery.
13. Risks of planned childbirth
- The author mentions concerns about artificially starting labor before the body is ready.
- Planned delivery may not always proceed as expected.
- Doctors must carefully consider the mother's and baby's conditions.
- The author suggests that medical intervention should have a clear reason.
14. The importance of natural labor
- The author discusses the difference between naturally starting labor and medically induced labor.
- The book questions whether scheduling childbirth for convenience is always appropriate.
- The author suggests that allowing labor to begin naturally can be preferable when there is no medical reason to intervene.
