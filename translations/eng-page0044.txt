Chapter 10: Morning Sickness (つわり)
1. When morning sickness begins
- Morning sickness usually begins around the fifth week of pregnancy or a little earlier.
- Pregnant women may experience nausea, vomiting, and general discomfort.
- Some women become extremely sensitive to smells.
- Foods they previously enjoyed may suddenly become unpleasant.
- Even thinking about certain foods can make them feel sick.
2. Changes in appetite
- Pregnant women may develop unusual food preferences.
- Some women suddenly dislike foods they previously enjoyed.
- Others may want to eat foods they normally avoid.
- Appetite may decrease considerably.
- The author explains that these changes are common during early pregnancy.
3. Morning sickness and the baby's health
- The author discusses the common belief that severe morning sickness indicates a healthy pregnancy.
- Some people believe that nausea is a sign that the baby is developing normally.
- However, the author warns that this belief is not always correct.
- The text says that a fetus may sometimes die even when the mother continues experiencing nausea.
- The author suggests that pregnancy symptoms alone cannot confirm the baby's health.
4. How long does morning sickness last?
- Morning sickness often improves around the 16th week of pregnancy.
- The author explains that symptoms generally become less severe as pregnancy progresses.
- However, some women experience nausea for a longer period.
- The author says that morning sickness usually does not cause serious harm when symptoms are mild.
5. Eating during morning sickness
- The author advises pregnant women not to force themselves to eat large meals.
- Eating small amounts more frequently may be easier.
- Women should choose foods they can tolerate.
- The author suggests foods such as:
  - Ice cream.
  - Sherbet.
  - Cold fruits.
  - Other light foods.
- The main goal is to maintain sufficient nutrition and hydration.
6. Preventing dehydration
- Repeated vomiting can cause dehydration.
- Pregnant women should try to drink enough fluids.
- The author recommends drinking water regularly.
- Keeping water near the bed may be helpful.
- If drinking a large amount causes nausea, small amounts may be easier to tolerate.
7. Morning sickness and empty stomach
- The author explains that nausea may become worse when the stomach is empty.
- Eating a small snack before getting out of bed may help.
- Crackers or other light foods are suggested.
- Women should avoid remaining hungry for long periods if hunger makes their nausea worse.
8. Medical treatment for morning sickness
- The author discusses medicines and other treatments for severe nausea.
- However, the text suggests that unnecessary medication should be avoided.
- The author mentions that some people use unusual methods or remedies to treat morning sickness.
- The author questions whether all these treatments are effective.
- Pregnant women with severe vomiting may need medical attention.
9. Traditional beliefs about morning sickness
- The author mentions the belief that a pregnant woman should eat whatever she desires.
- Some people believe that food cravings indicate what the baby needs.
- However, the author questions this idea.
- Food preferences during pregnancy do not necessarily indicate specific nutritional deficiencies.
- The author encourages a practical approach to eating.

Chapter 11: Minor Problems During Pregnancy (小さな異変)
10. Dizziness and fainting
- Some pregnant women experience dizziness, especially during early pregnancy.
- Dizziness may occur when standing up suddenly.
- The author explains that pregnancy causes changes in blood circulation.
- Blood pressure may fall temporarily when a woman stands.
- This can reduce blood flow to the brain and cause dizziness.
- The author advises pregnant women to stand up slowly.
11. Changes in blood circulation
- During pregnancy, the uterus becomes larger.
- The growing uterus may affect blood circulation.
- Lying flat on the back may sometimes cause discomfort.
- The author suggests lying on the side to improve comfort.
- Changes in blood flow may also contribute to feelings of weakness or dizziness.
12. Constipation during pregnancy
- Constipation is common during pregnancy.
- The author explains that bowel movements may become less frequent.
- Pregnancy-related changes can make digestion slower.
- Reduced physical activity may also contribute to constipation.
- The author recommends avoiding unnecessary use of laxatives.
13. Preventing constipation
- The author recommends eating fresh fruits and vegetables.
- Yogurt is also mentioned as a helpful food.
- Drinking enough water may help prevent constipation.
- Regular physical activity can support normal bowel movements.
- The author encourages pregnant women to maintain healthy eating habits.
14. When to seek medical attention
- The author explains that many minor pregnancy symptoms are not dangerous.
- However, some symptoms may require medical examination.
- Severe or persistent discomfort should not be ignored.
- Women should seek medical advice if symptoms become unusual or difficult to manage.
- The discussion continues on the next page.

Main message of this page
The author explains that morning sickness is common during early pregnancy and usually improves with time. Eating small meals, drinking enough fluids, and avoiding an empty stomach may help.

The next chapter introduces minor pregnancy problems such as dizziness and constipation, explaining how they develop and how women can manage them.

Important medical context: Persistent vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, reduced urination, or significant weight loss during pregnancy may indicate hyperemesis gravidarum, which requires medical assessment. Modern pregnancy-safe treatments for nausea are available and should not be avoided when medically necessary.
