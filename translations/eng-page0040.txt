Chapter 7: When Should You Visit the Doctor During Pregnancy?
1. How doctors calculate pregnancy duration
- Doctors calculate the age of a fetus to understand its development and decide when medical care is needed.
- In obstetrics, pregnancy is counted from the first day of the woman's last menstrual period, not from the day of fertilization.
- Pregnancy normally lasts about 280 days (40 weeks) using this method.
- The expected delivery date is calculated by adding nine months and seven days to the first day of the last menstrual period.
- For example, if the last period began on January 1, the expected delivery date would be around October 8.
2. How to calculate the expected delivery date
- The book explains a traditional Japanese method of counting pregnancy months.
- In this method, one pregnancy month is considered to be 28 days.
- Therefore, 10 pregnancy months equal 280 days.
- The author says that a woman's menstrual history is useful for calculating the expected delivery date.
- However, the actual delivery date may differ from the calculated date.
3. Confirming pregnancy through medical tests
- Pregnancy can be confirmed by detecting a hormone called hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin).
- This hormone begins increasing after pregnancy starts.
- The author explains that pregnancy can be confirmed by testing for this hormone.
- However, the book states that pregnancy testing was generally performed about four to five weeks after ovulation.
4. Women with irregular menstrual cycles
- Women with regular menstrual cycles can usually estimate their expected delivery date more easily.
- Women with irregular cycles may find it difficult to know when ovulation occurred.
- This makes calculating the expected delivery date less accurate.
- The author recommends keeping a record of menstrual periods.
- Women planning pregnancy should write down the dates of their periods every month.
5. Importance of keeping a menstrual calendar
- The author encourages women to maintain a menstrual calendar.
- Recording menstrual dates helps women understand their menstrual cycles.
- It can also help doctors estimate pregnancy duration.
- The author suggests that women who are planning to become pregnant should begin tracking their cycles before conception.
6. When should a pregnant woman visit the doctor?
- The author says that a woman should not delay visiting a doctor if she suspects pregnancy.
- Some women may wait until their pregnancy becomes obvious.
- Others may believe that medical examinations are unnecessary during the early stages.
- However, the author explains that medical care can help identify possible complications.
- Early medical consultation is particularly important if unusual symptoms occur.
7. Pregnancy symptoms and possible warning signs
- Some women experience very few symptoms during early pregnancy.
- Others may notice changes in their bodies.
- The author mentions symptoms that may require medical attention:
  - Unusual vaginal discharge.
  - Changes in the breasts.
  - Abnormal urination.
  - Other unexpected physical symptoms.
- The author emphasizes that pregnancy symptoms can differ between women.
8. Measuring basal body temperature
- The author discusses basal body temperature (BBT) as a way to identify possible pregnancy.
- Basal body temperature is the body's temperature when completely at rest.
- Women can measure it every morning immediately after waking.
- During the menstrual cycle, body temperature changes because of hormonal activity.
- After ovulation, body temperature usually increases slightly.
- If pregnancy occurs, the higher temperature may continue.
- The author says that a high-temperature phase lasting more than two weeks may indicate pregnancy.
- However, temperature measurements alone cannot confirm pregnancy.
9. Fear and anxiety about childbirth
- The author explains that women who are pregnant for the first time may feel anxious about childbirth.
- They may imagine that labor will be extremely painful.
- Some women become frightened because they do not know what childbirth will be like.
- The author suggests that understanding pregnancy and childbirth can help reduce anxiety.
- Learning about childbirth before delivery may make women feel more prepared.
10. Choosing between hospital and home delivery
- The author discusses how some women may prefer giving birth at home.
- In the past, home deliveries were more common.
- However, the author notes that hospital delivery became increasingly common.
- Some women may prefer home delivery because they feel more comfortable in familiar surroundings.
- The author says that the choice of delivery location may depend on family circumstances and available medical care.
11. Importance of family support
- The author explains that support from family members can help pregnant women feel more comfortable.
- Women may feel anxious if they are away from their family during childbirth.
- Some women prefer returning to their parents' home for delivery.
- The author mentions that this was a common practice in Japan.
- Having supportive family members nearby may reduce emotional stress.
12. Choosing a hospital during pregnancy
- The author advises pregnant women not to choose a hospital that is too far from home.
- Travelling long distances for regular checkups can be inconvenient.
- Women with kidney problems or diabetes may need special medical attention.
- Such women may benefit from treatment at a larger general hospital.
13. The first pregnancy checkup
- Many women feel nervous during their first pregnancy examination.
- Some women may be asked to provide a urine sample before seeing the doctor.
- The author advises women to avoid urinating immediately before their appointment if a urine sample will be needed.
- Women should arrive early and follow the hospital staff's instructions.
- If they are unsure about anything, they should ask the nurse.
14. Maternal and Child Health Handbook
- In Japan, pregnant women can receive a Maternal and Child Health Handbook from their local government office.
- This handbook records important information about pregnancy and childbirth.
- It includes details about:
  - Pregnancy checkups.
  - The mother's health.
  - The baby's birth and development.
  - Vaccinations.
  - The child's growth and health.

- The author encourages pregnant women to obtain this handbook as early as possible.
- It is useful for doctors and other healthcare professionals.
- It also helps parents keep track of their child's health.

Main message of this page
The author explains how pregnancy duration and delivery dates are calculated, how pregnancy can be confirmed, and why tracking menstrual cycles is useful. The page also discusses early medical consultation, basal body temperature, anxiety about childbirth, and choosing where to give birth.

Important medical context: Modern pregnancy tests can often detect hCG around the time of a missed period, earlier than the timeframe described in the book. Ultrasound during early pregnancy is generally more accurate for estimating gestational age than menstrual dates alone, particularly for women with irregular cycles.
